Title: A sneak peek at the next MyTailLog: try the new design on the web now

I’ve been working on a substantial MyTailLog update, based on how owners actually use their logbooks: checking what’s due, finding the entry behind a number, and getting a record in while standing next to the aircraft.

The new design lays the aircraft out like a binder. Status, Logbooks, Maintenance, Aircraft, Documents and Share keep related tasks together. You can follow maintenance history on a logbook map, open an inspection’s linked entry and scan, and check AI-read fields beside the original page. Add takes you straight to the form for an oil top-off, a flight reading, a squawk or a document. Ask your logbook is there when you want to search in plain English, with links back to the entries.

You can try the web redesign now: on a computer, open an aircraft and choose **Try the new design** in the Account navigation.

The redesigned native iPhone/iPad and Android apps are **coming soon**. Here’s a sneak peek at the layouts we’re testing: a focused workspace on your phone, more room beside the records on tablets, and iPhone Duo support that opens from a compact cover screen into two panes with an entry beside the logbook map. The Duo work has been checked in the simulator; physical-device validation is still ahead.

The native previews keep synced aircraft details and maintenance records on your phone and queue readings, oil, squawks and scans until you reconnect. Managed offline scan downloads are not yet included in those previews. We’re testing these builds through TestFlight and Play internal testing before the public rollout. I’ll share more as they get closer—there’s no public release date yet.

The existing public apps remain available on both stores, using the same account and aircraft. Google/Apple sign-in on the web and the MyFlightBook integration remain available.

MyTailLog is free and open source. Shared-key AI reading has a daily allowance. The physical logbooks remain the legal maintenance record; extracted entries need your review, and the app doesn’t determine airworthiness.
