The approved direction was authored at 390 pt and holds unchanged. Under 700 pt (Split View beside ForeFlight, Slide Over) the phone layout is used as-is. Frames are 1194 × 834, an 11" iPad in landscape, at 390pt-scale values.

What this fixes

The current app treats the iPad as a large phone: one column, a tab bar, and a Status screen that stretches four items across 1194 points. Pilots live on the iPad — it is in every flight bag and on every hangar bench — and the desk work MyTailLog exists for (reviewing an annual against the scan, walking an inspector through the ADs) is exactly what a tablet is for.

Every composition here is the phone's own pieces placed side by side. The verdict ring, the meter tiles, the attention card, the All-items rows, the page grid, the entry cards, the vault list — none is redrawn. That is what makes regular width a layout workstream: Sidebar and TwoPane compose, the screen components do not change.

The sidebar keeps the four destinations, adds Ask, and puts the fleet switcher where the header's tail number was on the phone — you still switch aircraft in place, you still keep the tab you were on. Keyboard shortcuts are shown beside the rows because a Magic Keyboard is common; nothing requires them.

Scans is the screen the iPad is for: page rail, scan, and entries all on screen, with ◎ ringing the read field on the scan. This is the web's proven review layout, not a new idea. Log gains the sanity check the phone never had room for — the last thirty readings beside the steppers, with edit and delete. Squawks gets resolution in-app, with "which entry cleared it" so the fix carries a signature.

The light appearance (13L) answers the handoff's open question #4: hangars are bright. Same token names, lighter values, every semantic colour still paired with its word. See the token table in the spec.
