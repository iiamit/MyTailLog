Paint Scheme on the web: records, aircraft, sharing

The quieter half of the app: reading forty-eight years of entries, keeping the aircraft's own facts straight, getting ready for the annual, and handing the records to someone else. The painted header and cheatline stay; the horizon appears only where something is coming due.

Reading the records

The owner is looking something up: when the prop was last overhauled, what the annual found, whether a stretch of years is missing. Today the timeline spends a whole screen on thirteen collapsed year rows, search is a small box in a sidebar, and Ask lives on a separate tab with one question at a time. Here the timeline opens on the aircraft's whole life with the gap drawn in, search and ask share the one field in the header, and every answer shows a crop of the page it came from.

The aircraft

The owner is keeping the machine's own facts straight: hours, oil, what is installed, what it weighs, the paperwork. Today these are five hub tabs, logging hours is the fifth section of Meters under a long ADS-B explainer, and equipment edits use browser prompt boxes. Under Aircraft each page opens with the one thing that needs doing, cross-links equipment to ADs and W&B, and checks the times written in the books against the times the app works out.

The annual and the records audit

The owner is getting ready to hand the aircraft and the books to the IA, or checking that the digitized records hold together. Today Annual prep and Records gaps are read-only dashboards that link away, findings cannot be dismissed, and nothing can be fixed in place. Here annual prep uses the horizon to show what falls due before the annual, and every loose end in the records carries the fix beside it.

Hand-offs

The owner is giving the records to someone: the IA before the annual, a buyer, an insurer, a co-owner. Today the share page asks for a free-text label and a list of checkboxes, links cannot be previewed or edited, the summary quietly includes reporter names, and the recipient gets bare tabs with a 500-entry cap. Here sharing starts from who it is for, the link shows what it exposes and who opened it, and the printed documents carry a footnote to the page behind every figure.

Data and account

The owner is moving records in or out, or adjusting how the app behaves. Today cloud backups and the AI allowance are buried in a 936-line profile page with six Save buttons, and reminders are off by default with nothing in the product saying so. Here backups live with export, where people look for them, and settings are one page with a side index, saving as each control changes.
