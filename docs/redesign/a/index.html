Direction A: Paint Scheme

The app wears the owner's aircraft. The header is painted in the plane's own colours, the tail number is the logo, and the cheatline under it becomes the one chart on the home screen: everything coming due, laid along the stripe.

The idea

Owners are attached to their aircraft, not to software. Every competitor dresses the app in the same navy cockpit; direction A dresses it in the owner's own airplane. At setup the owner picks the paint scheme (presets for common factory schemes, or two colours taken from a photo), and the header, the tail-number wordmark and the stripe under it take those colours. N734DM wears petrol and capri with a gold pinstripe; the Cherokee in the same hangar wears burgundy.

The stripe is not decoration. On the home screen it becomes the horizon: twelve months laid along the cheatline, with date-limited items riding the dark stripe and hour-limited items riding the light one, hours projected to dates from recent flying. One look answers "what is coming, and is it a date or hours that bites first".

Best for
Owners who check status often and want the app to feel like theirs; the most glanceable of the three.
Density
Low to medium. One main column, large type, generous white.
Signature
The cheatline horizon on Today, the due list and Pick a date.
Honest risks
Owner-chosen colours can fight contrast, so the system darkens or lightens a picked colour until text on it passes 4.5:1, and status colours never come from the paint. A crowded year can overload the horizon; flags group by week and the list below remains the full record.

Archivo, expanded, sets the tail number and headings, the way registration letters are painted wide on a fuselage. Atkinson Hyperlegible Next (drawn by the Braille Institute for low-vision readers) carries everything else at 17px, and its mono cut sets only meter readings, so numbers that are compared line up. Nothing on the web drops below 14px.

Status is always a round mark plus a word: a check, a clock, a warning sign, or a dash for "needs information". Kind tags say which limit applies ("Date", "Hours"). Every derived value carries a page chip, dashed when the entry is still only read by AI.

Navigation

Web: the painted header holds the aircraft switcher, five destinations (Today, Logbooks, Maintenance, Aircraft, Share), one Search-or-ask field and one Add button. Phone: Today, Logbooks, a centred Add, Due and More. Settings live behind the avatar. The full mapping from today's fifteen sidebar items is on the overview page.

What changes in the workflows

  • Status is a sentence, then a stripe, then a short list of what needs the owner, each with an action in the row.
  • Add is one button in the header and the thumb of the phone: log a flight (photograph the tach), scan pages, add oil, squawk, document, write an entry.
  • Review is a desk: the page large on the left, one field at a time on the right, the least certain first, its ink outlined in the scheme's gold.
  • Share starts from the person (your IA, a buyer, an insurer) and builds the right link or document.
