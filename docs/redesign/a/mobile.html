Getting in

The owner signs in once and lands in a hangar where each aircraft wears its own paint and says its one-line verdict. Today the fleet card reads "CURRENT" with no timestamp, and a second aircraft cannot be added on the phone at all. Here the sync time sits under the title, and adding an aircraft is a sheet with a registry lookup and a paint scheme.

A glance at the aircraft

Standing at the aircraft, the owner wants one sentence and the next few items in order. Today's phone status spends its first screen on a countdown ring, shows no "as of" time, and hides offline or pending changes once inside an aircraft. Here the header always carries the sync state, the stripe stands on its side so lapsed items sit above the gold "today" line, and the home changes with the aircraft's situation.

After a flight

Back on the ramp, the owner logs the flight with one thumb. Today that means finding Log in the tab bar and nudging a stepper whose text field turns "24" into "2.0", with no way to change the date. Here the Add button opens a sheet, the camera reads the hour meter into a draft, and the owner reviews the number instead of retyping it. After one Save, the phone says what the flight moved.

Something is wrong

During a preflight the owner notices an oil seep and wants it on record before forgetting. Today a squawk is text only, severity defaults to Low, and showing the mechanic means leaving the app. Here the photo comes first, the words can be dictated, severity is a deliberate choice, and the squawk goes to the mechanic as a private link.

Scanning at the hangar

With the logbooks open on the workbench, the owner photographs a stack of pages. Today the camera is five taps away, the sheet has no close button, and nothing says when pages have been read, so review starts from a different place later. Here the scan starts from Add, the queue shows each stage with today's reading allowance, and the phone announces when the pages are read.

Review on the phone

The owner reviews what was read, one page at a time, with the page filling the screen. Today the drawer covers most of the scan, the spotlight button sits at the far edge, and each page takes five taps to confirm and move on. Here the least-certain field opens first with its ink lit on the page, each field has three outcomes, and the next page is one swipe away.

What is due

The owner checks what is coming due and records what was done. Today "All items" and Mark done live outside navigation, so Back exits to the fleet, ADs sit two levels deep, and the three vocabularies for the same state disagree. Here the due list is a tab, ordered along the stripe with the gold today line, each item names the limit that applies, and Mark done is a sheet that can close two items at once.

The records

The owner looks something up: when the engine was overhauled, what the last annual covered, which years are missing. Today there is no global search, the Ask citations are buttons that do nothing, and entry detail is read-only in an older style. Here Logbooks is one tab with books and a timeline, the search icon in the header asks and searches in one field, and every answer opens its page.

Aircraft and documents

At the FBO or the insurer's desk the owner needs a number or a paper. Today equipment and directives hide two levels under Status, the meter tiles cannot be tapped, and a missing AROW document cannot be added from the card that reports it. Here the aircraft page holds hours with their sources, a written-versus-calculated check, equipment and W&B; documents lead with the carry-aboard card and the expiry dates.

Sharing

A buyer's mechanic asks for the records. Today "Share a records link" leaves the app and the share row in the account menu never renders. Here Share starts from who it is for, builds the right record room, and hands back a link with a QR code, an expiry and a way to revoke it.

Sync and settings

Most of the time sync is a line in the header. When something cannot upload, the owner needs to see it from inside the aircraft and decide. Today the conflict screen uses 10 to 13px type, Back always lands on the fleet, and appearance takes five taps to reach. Here both are one tap from More, at full size.

Tablet

On an iPad at the kitchen table the owner reviews pages and plans the shop visit. Today parts of the tablet app are a stretched phone, with a 640px fleet column and no counts in the sidebar. Here the tablet uses the web header with the five destinations, and splits into two or three panes so the list and its detail sit side by side.
