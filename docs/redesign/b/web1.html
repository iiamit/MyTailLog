Section Tabs on the web: status, capture and review

Getting in

The owner signs in, finds their aircraft on the shelf, and adds a new one in three short screens that end with a first verdict. Today's onboarding asks for the meters twice, says "three logbooks" while making five, and opens on a demo aircraft that pushes the real first action to the end of the grid. Here the hangar leads with each aircraft's one-line verdict, the add screen names all five books, and setup is one screen with three answers.

What is due

The owner opens the binder to find out where things stand, then works the list: marking items done, checking a date ahead of a trip, keeping ADs straight. Today the status page is read-only and hides below 1536px, the forecast and the AD page disagree on what counts, and nothing can be marked done where it is read. Here Status opens on a sentence, the Maintenance tab holds the one list with both limits and a "Mark done" on every row, and each due date carries the page it was counted from.

Squawks

The owner notes something wrong, keeps an eye on it, and hands it to a mechanic. Today a squawk is one line of text with a severity, resolved by one click with no note, date or entry, and absent from the overview. Here each squawk carries photos, a three-step severity in the owner's terms, a link a mechanic can open without an account, and, once fixed, the logbook entry that cleared it.

From paper to index

The owner turns a shelf of paper into confirmed entries: pages go in, the AI reads them in the background, and the owner checks each reading against its page. Today that spans four screens with no hand-offs, reading is a manual step tied to an open tab, the daily allowance is hidden in Profile, and review is split across two screens with different tools. Here the Logbooks tab holds one pipeline with a visible queue, and one review desk where the scan always sits on the right leaf.
