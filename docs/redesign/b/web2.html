Section Tabs on the web: records, aircraft and sharing

The rest of the binder. Every fact on these screens sits on a white leaf beside the page that proves it, and every hand-off starts from who will read it.

Reading the records

The owner is looking something up: when the prop was last overhauled, what an entry actually says, whether a gap is real. Today the timeline spends a screen on thirteen collapsed year rows, search is a small sidebar field, and Ask is a separate tab with no history. Here the Logbooks tab opens on a whole-life strip with the 1983 to 1991 gap in plain sight, search and Ask are one field at the foot of the rail, and whatever is selected brings its page onto the right-hand leaf.

The aircraft

The owner is checking what the aircraft is: its hours, its engine, what is bolted in, what it weighs, and the papers that go with it. Today logging hours is the fifth section of Meters under a long ADS-B explainer, oil top-offs sit on another tab, and equipment removal is a browser prompt that asks for a typed date. Under the green Aircraft tab each subject gets one leaf, the suggestion that needs an answer comes first, and the page or document behind each figure stays on the right.

The annual and the records audit

The owner is getting ready to hand the aircraft and the books to the IA. Today Annual prep is three read-only panels that link away, the gap audit cannot be dismissed or acted on, and duplicates live on a third screen. Here both are Maintenance sub-tabs where every finding carries its fix: book the visit, review the pages, accept the corrected reading, or say why a gap is real.

Hand-offs

The owner is giving the records to someone: an IA, a buyer, an insurer, a co-owner. Today Share opens on a form that asks "Who is it for?" as free text, links cannot be edited or extended, the owner cannot see what a link exposes, and the recipient gets raw image tabs with a 500-entry cap. Here Share starts from the audience and builds the right room, the owner sees exactly what each link shows and who opened it, and the recipient gets the same binder, read-only, with the gaps stated plainly.

Data and account

The owner is bringing records in from elsewhere, taking them out, or setting how the app behaves. Today Profile is one long scroll of eight cards with six Save buttons, the AI allowance and cloud backups are buried in it, and export sits on a separate page. Here the account button at the foot of the rail opens a grey board with two sub-tabs: settings on two leaves, and everything about data in and out on one screen.
