Section Tabs on the phone and tablet

Getting in

Dana opens the binder's cover, signs in once, and lands in the hangar, where each aircraft states in one sentence what its records show. Today the fleet card reads "7 tracked; 0 need more information", the sign-in shows confirmation messages in the error red, and a second aircraft cannot be added from the phone at all. Here adding one is a sheet that fills itself from the FAA registry.

A glance at the aircraft

The Status tab opens on a sentence, then the items behind it, each with the page that backs it. Today the phone spends its first screen on a countdown ring, repeats the same item four times, and never says when it last synced or that changes are waiting. Here the sync time sits under the tail number on every screen, offline work is listed where it applies, and the home gets quieter when there is nothing to do.

After a flight

Back at the tie-down, Dana photographs the tach and checks the number the phone read. Today logging hours means finding the Log tab, nudging a stepper whose typed value jumps to the wrong number, and saving with no date control and no word on what changed. Here the photo does the reading, the stepper is for the last tenth, and the saved screen says what the flight moved.

Something is wrong

During the walk-around Dana finds a cracked lens. Today a squawk is text only, severity defaults to Low, and swipe-to-resolve fires with no undo; there is no way to show the mechanic. Here the photo comes first, the words can be spoken, and the squawk becomes a link the mechanic can open without an account.

Scanning at the hangar

Dana found six loose airframe sheets from 1992 in the aircraft's folder and scans them where they lie. Today that takes five taps to reach the scanner because Records always opens on Documents, and after upload nothing says when the pages are read. Here Scan is in the Add list, the queue shows every page's state and today's reading allowance, and a notification closes the loop.

Review on the phone

The page fills the screen and the entry rides in a drawer below it, least certain field first. Today the drawer covers 62% of the page, the spotlight button sits at the far edge, and each page takes Confirm, Mark reviewed, Hide, swipe and Review. Here tapping a field lights its ink on the page, every field has the same three outcomes, and one button confirms the page and turns to the next.

What is due

The full list, an item with the page that proves it, and the ways to close it. Today "All items" and "Mark done" live outside navigation, so Back from them exits to the fleet; ADs are two levels below a row at the bottom of Status; and Mark done uses an older, smaller style. Here every item is a pushed screen with its page, marking done is a sheet, and the VOR check asks for exactly what 91.171(d) asks for.

The records

The Logbooks tab floods chrome yellow: the books, their coverage by year, every entry in time, and one field that searches and answers. Today search lives in three separate places, Ask is the third row under the vault, and its citations are buttons that do nothing. Here every answer quotes the page in the paper's own type, and the quote opens the page.

Aircraft and documents

The Aircraft tab holds the machine: meters, time since overhaul, equipment and weight and balance, with the Documents vault one level down in its own sienna. Today meter tiles are read-only, W&B and oil hide under "More records", and a missing AROW document cannot be added from the card that reports it.

Sharing

Share starts from who will read it. Today the phone can only send a text summary; a records link means leaving for the website, and "Share this aircraft" is in the code but never shown. Here the audience picks the right package, and the link comes back with a QR code to show across the hangar, an expiry and a way to revoke it.

Sync and settings

Offline work and the account. Today the waiting-to-upload screen uses 10.5 to 13.5px type, shows raw column names in conflicts, and is only reachable from the fleet; Appearance takes five taps to reach from inside an aircraft. Here the waiting list opens from the sync line on any screen, conflicts read as sentences, and settings are one tap from the tail switcher.

Tablet

On a tablet the binder opens flat: the coloured rail returns on the left and the two leaves sit side by side, record and source. Today the iPad is a stretched phone in places, with a 640px fleet column and a sidebar missing the counts it was designed with. Here every tab carries its count, and review gets a third pane for the pages.
