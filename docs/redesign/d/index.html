Direction D: Tabbed Binder

Direction B's records binder, with direction C's route map bound into it. The tabs, the coloured boards and the record-beside-its-page spread stay. The line map becomes the binder's index by time: a spine across the top of every section, a full map of the aircraft's life in Logbooks, and the line ahead for planning what comes due.

The idea

A binder is good at holding the records and proving each fact: open the tab, and the page is there. It is bad at time. Fifty years sit flattened into sections, and nothing in a binder shows what comes next. A line map is the opposite: it shows the whole life and the road ahead at a glance, but on its own it is a picture rather than a record.

Direction D puts them together. Each logbook is still a tab and a coloured line at the same time. The tabs answer "where is it kept", the lines answer "when did it happen, and what is next", and every stop on a line opens the page that proves it on the right-hand leaf, exactly as a row does in B.

Keeps from B
The cover rail and tabs sized by content, section boards, white leaves, the page on the dark mat, the paper-voice and app-voice type rule, ink-and-vermilion status.
Takes from C
Lines for books, stops for entries, interchanges for multi-book visits, dashed gaps, dotted futures and rings for what is due.
New in D
The spine on every board, the line ahead with proposed shop visits, and the stop-to-page link.
Honest risks
Two systems on one screen can crowd, so the map is drawn in ink on a white leaf and never on the coloured board, and the spine stays one strip high. Line inks and section colours are separate sets; the map never uses a section colour.

Home, on the web and on the phone

Three places the map lives

The spine, on every board

A strip one finger high under every section's title, drawn from the same data as the map. It brackets what the current screen covers (the year ahead on Status, a decade on a filtered list, one stop on an entry), so the owner always knows where in time they are. Clicking anywhere on it jumps the map to that moment.

The map, in Logbooks

The whole life of the aircraft. Stops open their pages on the right leaf; an interchange opens every page signed in that visit, one tab per book. A dashed gap offers to scan the missing pages. Filters narrow it to one book; dragging across a stretch filters the list below to those years.

The line ahead, in Status

The same lines continued into the future, with due items as rings in B's status marks: black ring current, vermilion ring due soon, solid vermilion overdue. Items that fit one shop visit are drawn as a proposed interchange. "Plan ahead" stretches it to 24 months and lets the owner drag a date to see what will have lapsed by then.

Type and status are B's unchanged: Libre Franklin for the app, Literata only for words transcribed from paper, black dot for current, vermilion ring for due soon, solid vermilion for overdue. The map adds one set of colours, printer's inks for the four books, deliberately darker and duller than the section colours so a line never reads as a tab and never reads as a status.

The map is always drawn in ink on a white leaf, never on a coloured board, so the board keeps its job of saying which section is open. On the phone the map turns vertical: one column per book, newest at the top.

Navigation

B's six tabs stay: Status, Logbooks, Maintenance, Aircraft, Documents, Share, with Search-or-ask, Add and the account at the foot of the rail. Two sub-tabs change. Status gains "Plan ahead", which replaces both Forecast and Pick a date. Logbooks opens on "Map" instead of a page list. The spine adds a second way to move: by time, from any section. On the phone the five coloured tabs stay, and the Logbooks tab opens on the vertical map.

What changes in the workflows

  • Planning is drawing. The line ahead shows due items on the books they belong to; dragging the date marker shows what lapses by then; accepting a suggested visit books it as a planned interchange that the reminders then follow.
  • Finding is travelling. Search results light up as stops on the map, so an answer shows where in fifty years it sits as well as which page it rests on.
  • Scanning fills the map. New pages land on their line as hollow stops while they are read, turn solid when confirmed, and a dashed gap shortens as its pages come in.
  • Hand-offs open with the map. The pre-buy dossier and the record room start with the aircraft's life as a line map, every gap labelled, before the tabbed sections.
  • Everything else is B. Screens not shown on this direction's pages work as in direction B, with the spine added under each board's title.
