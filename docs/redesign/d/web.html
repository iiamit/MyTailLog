Tabbed Binder on the web

Twenty desk screens where the route map changes what the owner sees: the spine under every board, the line ahead for planning, the map for browsing fifty years of pages, and the map as the cover of every hand-off. Screens not drawn here (sign in, the due list, pages library, write an entry, equipment, weight and balance, documents, settings and the rest) work as in direction B, with the spine added under each board's title.

Getting in

The owner picks an aircraft from the shelf, or starts one from nothing. In B each binder showed how full its sections were; in D each also shows its life as a strip, so a gap in the records or a visit coming up is visible before the binder is opened. A new aircraft with no pages gets the same map, honestly empty: the past drawn dashed as "no pages yet", today marked, and the year ahead worked out from the three setup answers.

What is due

The owner reads where things stand and plans the next visits. In B this was a table and a separate "Pick a date" chart that disagreed with the forecast. In D the same lines that hold the past run on into the future: due items are rings on their book's line, items that fit one visit are drawn as a proposed interchange, and a date marker dragged along the line answers "what will have lapsed by then" in place. Item details and ADs show their own history as stops, so a late renewal is something you can see.

The records

The owner looks something up: when the engine was overhauled, what a decade of annuals says, whether the 1983 to 1991 gap is real. B opened Logbooks on a density strip and a list; D opens it on the route map, where every stop is an entry and clicking one puts its page on the right leaf. Filtering to one book, zooming to a decade and selecting a gap are all the same map, and an entry reached from a stop keeps a "previous and next stop on this line" control so the owner can walk a book in time order.

From paper to index

The owner turns paper into confirmed entries: pages go in, the AI reads them in the background, and the owner checks each reading against its page. B made this one pipeline and one review desk. D adds where each page lands: pages land on their book's line as hollow stops while they are read and unconfirmed, turn solid when the owner confirms them, and a dashed gap visibly shortens as its pages come in. The first book read becomes a line with what the reader found pinned to it.

The aircraft

The owner checks the engine's hours and the meters behind every countdown. B put these in tables beside the pages they come from. D draws the engine's life since overhaul as its own stretch of line, from the 2005 overhaul to the TBO advisory about 2030, with every oil change a stop, so the rhythm of maintenance and the distance left are one picture.

Hand-offs

The owner hands the records to someone else: the IA for the annual, a buyer, an insurer. B made every hand-off start from the audience and kept the page behind every number. D opens every hand-off with the map. The IA sees the run-up to the annual as a line, the record room's cover is the aircraft's life with its gap labelled, and the printed pre-buy dossier begins with that same map, every gap and overhaul footnoted to its page.
