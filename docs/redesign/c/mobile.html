Getting in

The owner signs in once and lands in the hangar, where each aircraft says in one sentence how it stands. Today the fleet card reads "7 tracked; 0 need more information", the only action on it is mislabelled, and a second aircraft cannot be added from the phone at all. Here adding an aircraft is a sheet that fills itself from the FAA registry.

A glance at the aircraft

Standing at the aircraft, the owner wants the answer in one line and the next few stops in arrival order. Today's phone spends the first screen on a countdown ring, never says when it last synced, and hides offline and conflict states once you are inside an aircraft. Here the sentence comes first, the sync time sits in the header of every aircraft screen, and the home changes with the aircraft's situation.

After a flight

Back on the ramp, the owner records the tach, and maybe a quart of oil, with one hand. Today that is buried under a tab, the value field fights typing, the date cannot be changed and is stored in UTC. Here the big Add button opens every kind of record, a photo of the tach becomes a draft flight to check rather than retype, and saving says which stops moved.

Something is wrong

The owner notices something on the walkaround and wants it on record before forgetting it. Today a squawk is text only, defaults to Low, and a swipe resolves it with no undo. Here the camera opens first, words can be dictated, severity is a deliberate choice, and the squawk goes to the mechanic as a link with the photo.

Scanning at the hangar

The owner photographs a stack of logbook pages, then puts the phone away. Today the scanner is five taps deep, nothing says when pages have been read, and review starts somewhere else. Here scanning starts from Add or from a book, the queue is a short line with four stops, and the phone announces when reading is finished.

Review on the phone

Review is checking what the AI read against the page, one entry at a time. Today the drawer covers most of the scan, the spotlight button sits at the screen edge, and each page takes five taps to finish. Here the page fills the screen, the drawer starts with the least certain field, its ink lights on the scan, and confirming a page moves to the next with one swipe. This is the midnight theme, which keeps the paper the brightest thing on screen.

What is due

The due board lists every stop in the order it arrives, documents included, with the limit that bites first. Today "All items" lives outside navigation, so Back exits the aircraft, and Mark done is an older screen of 10px type. Here every item opens to its page, marking done is a sheet, related items can be done together, and a date can be picked to see what will have lapsed by then.

The records

On the phone the records are for looking something up for a mechanic or a buyer. Today history uses text glyphs in hard-coded colours, entry detail is read-only with no way back to its page, and Ask is three levels down with citations that cannot be tapped. Here the books, the timeline, each entry beside its scan, and one search-or-ask field share the same line colours and page chips.

Aircraft and documents

At the aircraft the owner checks a meter, an installed part, or that the carry-aboard paperwork is in the vault. Today equipment is two levels under Status, the meter tiles cannot be tapped, and a missing AROW document cannot be added from where it is reported missing. Here the aircraft is one page under More, and documents with an expiry join the due board.

Sharing

The owner is standing with the IA, a buyer or an insurer and wants to hand over the right records. Today "Share a records link" leaves the app and the Share row in the account menu never renders. Here Share starts with who it is for, builds the matching record room, and ends on a QR code to show across the wing.

Sync and settings

Everything recorded on the phone is kept on the phone until it reaches the server, and the owner can always see what is waiting. Today the waiting list and conflicts use 10px type, show only from the hangar, and Appearance is five taps away from an aircraft. Here a conflict compares "yours" and "theirs" in plain words, and settings are one tap under More.

Tablet

On a tablet the app takes the desk layout: the slim rail and command field, with a second pane so a list and its detail sit side by side. Today the iPad fleet home is a stretched 640px phone column, and three-way review exists but without counts or a sync line. Here review gets three panes, pages, scan and fields, and works with a keyboard attached.
