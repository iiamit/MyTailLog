Line Diagram on the web: records, aircraft and sharing

Every screen keeps a thin strip of the four logbook lines under the header, with today marked and the thing on screen ringed, so the owner always knows where in the aircraft's life they are. Lists run in date order; the diagram is the way in.

Reading the records

The owner is looking something up: when the engine was last overhauled, what the annual found, whether a run of years is missing. Today the timeline spends a whole screen on thirteen collapsed year rows, search is a small box in a sidebar, and Ask is a separate tab that answers one question with no page to look at. Here the timeline is the diagram itself, drawn to scale with the 1983 to 1991 gap in plain view, and the command field in the header both searches and answers, with a crop of every page an answer rests on.

The aircraft

The owner is checking the machine rather than the paperwork: what the meters read, how the engine is wearing, what is installed, what it weighs, where the certificates are. Today these live on six hub tabs in three groups, logging hours is the fifth section of Meters under a long ADS-B explainer, and delete and transfer hide on the sharing page. Here they are six tabs under one destination, Aircraft, each led by the fact that matters, with suggestions from the logs and from ADS-B offered to confirm, never written for the owner.

The annual and the records audit

The owner is getting ready to hand the aircraft and the books to the IA, or checking that the digitized record holds together. Today Annual prep and Records gaps are read-only dashboards: every row jumps to the top of another page, nothing can be settled in place, and a finding links to nothing. Here annual prep draws the route from today to the annual with every stop on it, suggests what to do on one visit, and each loose end carries its own action. The records check offers a fix beside every finding and asks before deleting anything.

Hand-offs

The owner is giving the records to someone: the IA before the annual, a buyer, an insurer, a co-owner. Today the record room form sits on a page that also holds transfer and delete, a link's contents can't be seen on its row, the app never sends the link, and the printed summary, dossier and records report disagree with each other. Here Share starts by asking who it is for and builds the right room from that, each live link shows what it holds, who opened it and when it ends, and the printed documents share one layout with every number footnoted to its page.

Data and account

The owner is moving records in or out, or adjusting how the app behaves. Today export lives on the aircraft, automatic backup in Profile and restore on the hangar, three places for one job, and Profile is one long scroll of eight unrelated cards with six Save buttons, reminders off by default. Here data has one page with import, export and scheduled backups together, and settings are split into short sections with a side list, each saving as it changes.
